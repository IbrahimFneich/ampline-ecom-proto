## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** The hero is owned by this product: the device picker fills a battery with the device's own published ceiling, names the connector, marks it indicative, and keeps watts on power products only. No unrelated store could lift it unchanged. Below #groups the page turns category-interchangeable: headline, category grid and featured row three times, then a brand strip, and none of it reacts to the chosen device beyond the query string on tile links. The thesis also breaks on its first promise: the hero copy says "cases", but the three model-specific cases carry an empty compat, so "Fits iPhone 15 or newer" never contains a case and the "1 cases & mounts" count is the Ampline dash mount.

**Deterministic scan.** detect.mjs on index.html: exit 2, 5 findings, all at line 0. design-system-radius 14px (the battery fill, recorded in DESIGN.md), two gpt-thin-border-wide-shadow on .rowbtn (styles.css:1039; the recorded floating-layer shadow), cramped-padding on the house section (styles.css:1092, padding-block 48px in the browser; the static scan misreads the logical property), tight-leading 1.2x on an element the scan could not locate and the browser did not reproduce. All five read as false positives or recorded intent. The detector caught nothing the review missed; the review caught everything material.

## Overall Impression

The first screen is the product: a question, an answer, a battery that fills. Everything after it is a competent catalogue tour that forgets the question was asked. The single biggest opportunity is to make the rest of the page answer for the chosen device the way the hero does, starting with the cases the hero promises.

## What's Working

- **The device-ceiling battery.** It reads devices[].max_charge_w, names the connector, and says "Indicative; each power product below states what its port can give." Honest and specific at the same time.
- **The empty-state battery.** With no device chosen it shows family ranges ("Phones 18 to 45W"), so the hero is never blank and the first tap has somewhere to go.
- **The honesty grammar.** Amber SYNTHETIC on every price, "W max per port" on power tiles only, no delivery or warranty claims anywhere. Applied without exception.

## Priority Issues

- **[P1] Cases never appear in a fit answer.** The hero and the fits-row copy promise cases, but the iPhone 16 Silicone Case, the Galaxy S25 Clear Case and the Smart Folio carry compat [] since the family list is too coarse to claim them. **Why it matters:** the thesis "start from what you own" fails on the most model-specific purchase a phone owner makes. **Fix:** either add model-level ids (iphone-16, galaxy-s25, ipad-air-11-m2) nested under their family buckets and ask "Which model?" only when a case is in play, or remove "cases" from #own-sub and #fits-sub until the data supports it. **Suggested command:** /impeccable shape (the device model question), then /impeccable clarify for the copy.
- **[P1] On phones the payoff is a green bar, not a product.** After a device tap the battery fills in view and the fits row sits a full screen below; at 320px nothing but chips and the glyph is visible. **Why it matters:** the primary audience is phone-first and the peak moment ends without a product. **Fix:** on phones collapse the result to one line ("11 fit · takes up to 30W over USB-C") and scroll to #fits, or move the first three fitting tiles into the hero result. **Suggested command:** /impeccable adapt.
- **[P1] The home battery misstates edge devices.** Dell XPS (130W) and MacBook Pro (140W) pin the 0 to 100W track full with no overflow mark, and the caption says "The Other Android phone's own charge ceiling, as its maker publishes it", inventing a maker for a catch-all bucket. The product page already stretches its axis and hides the glyph for catch-alls; home does not. **Why it matters:** the one number the page is trusted for reads wrong on four devices. **Fix:** reuse the product page rules in app.js: axis max(100, ceiling) with the scale relabelled, and "A typical figure for this group" for catch-all ids. **Suggested command:** /impeccable harden.
- **[P2] The product photos cost more than the page.** Five PNG renders weigh 0.6 to 1.3 MB each and display at 206px; the home page now ships several megabytes of images on a market where PRODUCT.md assumes phones and uneven connections. **Why it matters:** the first paint on a Lebanese mobile connection is the first impression. **Fix:** re-encode to WebP or JPEG at 800px with a 2x variant, keep PNG only where transparency is needed and trim its margins; serve via srcset. **Suggested command:** /impeccable optimize.
- **[P2] Photo tiles and drawn tiles read as two stores.** A photo on a white well sits next to three Ampline drawings on grey wells, so the house brand looks like the placeholder. There is also no trust row on home although PRODUCT.md names it a category convention and the truck, shield and card symbols sit unused in index.html. **Why it matters:** mixed wells undercut the house brand the page is meant to sell; a missing trust row leaves cash-on-delivery, the local norm, unmentioned. **Fix:** one well treatment for both (grey well, equal padding, photos contained), and a three-item trust row under the hero result with amber "to be confirmed" labels instead of invented terms. **Suggested command:** /impeccable polish for the wells, /impeccable layout for the trust row.
