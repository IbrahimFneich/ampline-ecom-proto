## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** skinned for The Charging Lock Screen, not written for "start from what you own". The world's marks are all here and done well: the count battery, dormant grey options, amber SYNTHETIC tags, one type family. Remove them and this is a generic store listing. The first viewport never asks for a device, Fits is the last group in the rail and the sheet, the search cannot read a device name, and the H1 stays "All products" when the list fits an iPhone 16. The two live product-specific ideas, would-be counts and fit state carried in the URL and tile links, are real and good, but they sit below the fold.

**Deterministic scan:** CLI scan of shop/all.html returned 1 finding: cramped-padding on div.toolbar (line 0, "children flush against border-bottom+bg on left"). In practice the toolbar sits inside the page gutter and the flush hairline row is the house pattern, so this is a candidate false positive. In-page detection (detect.js injected via the live server, five views): 1440 default and 1440 Fits iPhone 16 each reported 16 cramped-padding findings, all on Fits chips (0px vertical padding for 14px text). The chips are 36px fixed-height flex boxes with a 21px line, so the text has room; false positive on padding, but the same chips are the ones under the 44px touch floor on phones, where the design review and the detector point at the same element. monotonous-spacing (16 of 16 at ~4px) and text-occlusion ("heading crowded against" 73% covered by span.tag) were produced by the detector's own overlays; a run without overlays did not report them. heading-rhythm on the tile h3 (8px above vs 29px below, "GaN 100W Desktop Charger", "3-in-1 100W Cable") is a real rhythm note the design review missed: the title binds to the tag row and floats from the spec below. 390 views reported only the toolbar finding. No page errors, no console errors in any view.

**Visual overlays:** the browser run was headless Playwright, so there is no [Human] tab and no user-visible overlay. Overlay screenshots are in the critique3-b scratch folder (a-fits-crop.png shows the chip outlines).

## Overall Impression

A calm, honest, well-built listing whose thesis is in the wrong place. Everything a shopper needs is here, labelled, counted and shareable, but the page opens on "All products" with a disclaimer and asks for the device last. On the phone, the one control that carries the store's promise breaks the sheet when tapped. The single biggest opportunity: let fit lead this page the way it leads home, and make the search understand a device name.

## What's Working

- **Would-be counts and dormant options.** Every option shows its result count and zero-result options go grey without disappearing. A shopper can predict a tap, and normal rail use almost never reaches an empty list.
- **Fit state travels.** The count line names the device, the URL carries search, group, brand, device and sort through reload, and tile links carry the device to the product page.
- **Honest materials with restraint.** SYNTHETIC tags, grey instead of red for dormant states, no logos, one family of type. Dense tiles stay readable.

## Priority Issues

- **[P0] The phone filter sheet breaks on a device tap.** A real tap on a visible Fits chip (label[for="dev-iphone-16"]) scrolls the dialog itself by 579px: the sheet head moves to -478px, the foot floats at y=196 and blank white fills the rest. Brand checkboxes do not trigger it. Cause: the visually-hidden radios are absolutely positioned against the fixed .sheet, and focusing one scrolls a dialog that has overflow: hidden. Why it matters: it is the store's thesis control on its primary device, and the same template serves every category sheet. Fix: `.sheet { overflow: clip }` (styles.css, the .sheet block near line 632); injected in the browser, the dialog's scroll stays at 0 and the head stays at 101px. Suggested command: /impeccable harden.
- **[P1] Search ignores fit and misses the device itself.** "iphone 16" returns only the case because the whole-number rule rejects "16" when a comma follows it ("Apple iPhone 16, 128 GB"). "iphone charger", "iphone 16 charger", "macbook charger", "type-c", "powerbank" and "65 w" each return 0; "usb c" returns 46 of 58; "case" ranks earbuds above cases. Why it matters: this page is the search landing, and these are the phrasings a shopper replacing a charger actually types. Fix: let a bare number match before a comma in matchesQuery; resolve device names and aliases in the query into the device filter so "iphone 16 charger" becomes Fits iPhone 16 plus "charger"; a small synonym table (type-c, powerbank, "65 w"); rank name matches above spec matches. Suggested command: /impeccable harden.
- **[P1] Fit does not lead on this page.** Fits is the last sheet group, starting around y=928 below 15 checkboxes; on desktop it sits about 1,000px down a rail that does not stick, and the H1 never reflects the device. The surface brief chose this order for a search landing, but it conflicts with PRODUCT.md principle 1. The all-products Price bands ($500, $1,500) are dead for an accessory shopper, and there is no Category facet to split chargers from cases. Fix: a compact "What do you own?" row above the grid, families first then devices, as home does; retitle to "Fits iPhone 16" when a device is set; recompute price bands from the current results or drop Price here. Suggested command: /impeccable layout, then /impeccable distill for the 25 flat chips.
- **[P1] Adding from a tile is fragile.** The cart event re-runs the whole render (shop.js, the ampline:cart listener): all 58 tiles re-animate, focus drops to the body, the button reads "In cart" within 120ms so "Added" never shows, and the #count live region re-announces. 41 SKUs are added with no colour or storage; the iPhone's cart line shows no colour. Fix: update only the clicked button and the cart badge; for SKUs with variants, label the tile button "Choose options" and link to the product page with the device, or add the default variant and show its label in the cart. Suggested command: /impeccable harden, then /impeccable polish.
- **[P2] The page does not hold the shopper's place.** After a header search the first row lands under the sticky toolbar (tiles at 122px against a toolbar ending at 209px on phone; 72 against 115 on desktop), the search chip scrolls away and the phone keyboard stays up. Back after a filter leaves the listing (replaceState); Back from a product page drops the scroll from 8,149 to 1,246. The empty state for Devices plus iPhone 16 removes the device to show 41 products, when removing Devices would keep the device and show 12. Fix: give #tiles a scroll margin equal to the sticky stack, blur the field on submit under a coarse pointer, use pushState for filters, never choose the device as the removal when another removal also yields results. Suggested command: /impeccable harden.

## Minor Observations

- Skeleton: the empty sort select collapses to a chevron-only pill on desktop, and "Loading…" shows with no battery.
- Tile h3 rhythm (detector): 8px above, 29px below; the title binds to the tag row rather than to its own spec.
- The Filters badge reads "Filters 1" without naming what is applied.
- Search miss: "Clear the search" and "Clear all filters" do the same thing when only a search is active.
- After a device pick, 7 of 9 brand rows sit dormant; the Brand group earns little on a fit answer.
- Cables read "W max per port" on the tile but "Max through the cable" on the product page.
- Page weight: first view 256 KB over 6 requests; a full scroll loads all 44 photos for 1.74 MB total (largest 100 KB). Fine for a prototype, worth pagination or a "show more" if the catalog grows.
